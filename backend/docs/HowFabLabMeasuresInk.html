How FabLab Measures Ink

The system has no fixed amount of ink per order. It looks at the customer's actual design and works out how much of each printer ink that design needs.

If you only remember one thing

“When a customer saves a design, the system measures the artwork. It finds what share of the printable area each of the four inks covers, multiplies that by the product's print area in square centimetres and by an ink rate, and gets millilitres. That amount is reserved from the ink bottles when the admin approves the order.”

ink (ml) = coverage % × printable area (cm²) × ink rate (ml per cm²) × quantity

From a saved design to the ink bottles

How it works, step by step

  1. The design studio exports the artwork

    When the customer saves a design, the studio draws their shapes, text and images by themselves on a transparent background, with no shirt or mug behind them. This “flat print” is sent to the server with the design.

  2. The server splits every pixel into the four inks

    A sublimation printer makes every colour from four inks: Cyan, Magenta, Yellow and Black (CMYK). The server converts each pixel's colour into those four. Red becomes magenta plus yellow, black is black ink only, and white needs no ink at all. A transparent pixel has nothing to print, and a half-transparent one counts half.

  3. It adds them up into a coverage percentage

    The totals are divided by the printable area, giving the share of the print each ink covers. For example, “magenta covers 5% of the printable area”. This is saved with the design.

  4. Percent becomes square centimetres, then millilitres

    Coverage × the product's printable area (a T-shirt is 2,400 cm², a mug 200 cm²) gives the square centimetres of solid ink. Times the ink rate (0.0025 ml per cm²) gives millilitres per item. Times the quantity gives the order's total.

  5. The ink moves in inventory like any other material

    When the admin approves the order, the millilitres are reserved from each ink bottle. When staff start production, the reservation becomes used. If the order is cancelled, the ink goes back. The system won't approve an order if a bottle doesn't have enough.

Worked example: 10 white T-shirts

The design is a red logo that covers 5% of the shirt's printable area, with black text covering 2%. The settings are the system's own: a White Cotton T-Shirt has a printable area of 2,400 cm² (front and back), and every ink's rate is 0.0025 ml per cm².

Magenta, for one shirt: 5% × 2,400 cm² = 120 cm², and 120 cm² × 0.0025 ml/cm² = 0.30 ml. The red logo uses magenta and yellow equally because red is 100% of each. The cyan bottle isn't touched.

On the admin's Review screen, under Materials Required, each ink bottle shows the working, like this:

White Cotton T-Shirt: 5% Magenta coverage of 2400 cm² (measured from the print, size M) × 0.0025 ml/cm² × 10 items
White Cotton T-Shirt: 0% Cyan coverage of 2400 cm² (measured from the print, size M) × 0.0025 ml/cm² × 10 items — none of this colour in the artwork

Transfer paper is measured the same way

The shop prints the artwork on an A4 transfer sheet (21 × 29.7 cm), cuts it out and presses it on. So the system draws a box around the artwork, adds a 0.5 cm cutting margin on every side, and counts that piece as a fraction of a sheet.

For a front print 12 × 14 cm: the cut piece is 13 × 15 cm = 195 cm². An A4 sheet is 623.7 cm², so one shirt takes 0.31 of a sheet, and 10 shirts take about 3.1 sheets. If a design is too big to fit on one sheet, the Review screen shows a warning before the admin approves.

If the panel asks

How does the system know how much ink an order uses?
It measures the customer's actual artwork. Every pixel is split into the four printer inks, and the share of the printable area each ink covers is turned into millilitres using the product's print area and the ink rate.
Why not a fixed amount of ink per order?
A small chest logo and a full-front photo would then use the same ink, and the inventory would drift from the real bottles. Measuring the design means a bigger or darker print takes more ink and a small one takes less.
Why four inks, and why CMYK?
The sublimation printer has four ink bottles, Cyan, Magenta, Yellow and Black, and mixes every colour from them. Screens show colour as red, green and blue light, so the system converts each pixel from RGB to CMYK, the same way a printer driver does.
Where did 0.0025 ml per cm² come from? Is it accurate?
It's a starting value: roughly 1.5 ml for a solid A4 sheet of one colour on a desktop sublimation printer. It's meant to be calibrated for the shop's own printer: print a solid 10 × 10 cm square of one colour, weigh the bottle before and after, and divide the millilitres used by 100. The admin enters the result for each ink under Customization Pricing.
Does a 2XL shirt use more ink than a Small?
No. The shop presses a cut transfer, and a 12 × 14 cm print is 12 × 14 cm on any shirt size. The ink depends on the design, not the size of the shirt.
What about white?
White uses no ink. Sublimation can't print white; white parts of a design show the white of the shirt or mug. That's why the system counts white as zero.
When is the ink deducted from the inventory?
It's reserved when the admin approves the order, and becomes “used” when staff start production. If the order is cancelled, the ink is returned. The system won't approve an order if a bottle doesn't have enough.
What if the computed amount is wrong?
The admin sees the full working on the Review screen and can change any figure before approving. The corrected amount is what gets reserved.
What about ink wasted on printer cleaning and test prints?
That isn't part of any order, so staff record it by hand in the Usage Log. The bottle's stock stays true to what's really left.
How exact is it?
It's an estimate from the artwork, not a measurement at the printer. Real printers vary with their colour settings and fabric. That's why the rate can be calibrated and the admin can correct each order. It's still far closer than a fixed amount, because it follows the actual design.
What about designs saved before this feature?
Those have no flat print, so the system estimates their ink from the shapes, text and images in the design. The Review screen labels them “estimated from the design” instead of “measured from the print”.

Show it live in a few minutes

  1. As a customer, customize a white T-shirt: add a red shape and some black text. Save it, add it to the cart and check out. Untick SMS for practice orders, so no text credits are spent.

  2. As admin, open Orders and click Review on that order.

  3. Scroll to Materials Required. Read out the magenta line, then click Show where the magenta prints to show the red parts lighting up.

  4. Open Customization Pricing to show the ink rates and the transfer paper, and Products → Edit to show the printable area.

  5. If you approve the demo order, show the reserved ink in Raw Materials → Usage Log, then cancel the order so the ink goes back.

The figures use the system's settings as of 26 November 2026: T-shirt printable area 2,400 cm², every ink 0.0025 ml/cm², A4 transfer paper with a 0.5 cm margin. If an admin changes them, the results change with them.
